**Interpretation.** Each node emits at a nominal **2 Hz** (0.5 s period). Delivery is bursty: a large share of consecutive frames share a timestamp and the p95 inter-arrival is ~2 s. The pattern is ~4 frames flushed together every ~2 s, which is characteristic of USB-serial buffering on the gateway rather than of the sensors themselves.

### Timestamp resolution degrades during the session

| item | value |
|---|---|
| duplicate `(node, ts_ms)` pairs | 2356 |
| rows sitting in a duplicated `(node, ts_ms)` group | 3839 of 6527 |

This is a **real defect worth fixing on the hardware side**: `seq` keeps incrementing correctly (no gaps, no resets) while `ts_ms` stalls, so distinct samples are being stamped with the same device time. Consequences accepted in this pipeline:

- Window-level **aggregate** statistics (mean, std, RMS, percentiles, kurtosis) stay valid -- they do not depend on intra-burst ordering.
- Window-level **slope / rate** features are only accurate to roughly the burst period (~2 s). They are kept, but should not be read as fine-grained derivatives.
- Per-sample event timing cannot be recovered from this file.

### Finding A -- the RPM channel is nearly constant and heavily quantised

`hall_rpm` spans only **20.37-20.69 RPM** (std 0.053, CV 0.26%) across the whole session and takes only **29 distinct values**. It also *holds* the same value for a median of **6 consecutive frames** (~3.0 s at 2 Hz), so its effective update rate is about **0.33 Hz**, not 2 Hz.

Consequences: the conveyor ran at **one essentially fixed speed for the entire recording**, within-window RPM std/slope features are dominated by quantisation rather than by real speed dynamics, and every `something / rpm` cross-feature is close to a rescaled copy of its numerator. Those features are still computed (they are the right features for a rig that does vary its speed) but they carry very little information *in this dataset*, and the near-duplicates among them are pruned before training.

### Finding B -- the session is a thermal warm-up transient, not steady state

Mean temperature rises from **31.42 degC** in the first 60 s to **36.73 degC** in the last 60 s (**+5.31 degC**, Pearson r with session time = **0.958**). Ambient rises too, but `temperature - ambient` still climbs from **3.43** to **6.42 degC** (r = **0.901**).

This is the single most important modelling constraint in the dataset. Absolute temperature is **confounded with elapsed session time**. Any purely chronological train/test split will therefore flag late windows as anomalous for a reason that has nothing to do with mechanical condition. The pipeline handles it explicitly: the deployed baseline is fitted over the whole session so the entire warm-up range is inside the baseline, and the chronological split is reported separately and only as a *drift diagnostic*.

### Finding C -- vibration and RPM are stationary over the session

`vibration_rms` first half 0.0672 g vs second half 0.0670 g (r with time = 0.045); `hall_rpm` 20.510 vs 20.530 (r = 0.225). Neither shows the strong monotone trend that temperature does.

### Finding D -- a handful of impulsive vibration frames

| `vibration_rms` | 0.0666 | 0.0058 | 2 | 1 | 11.2 |

A small number of frames show large crest factor and kurtosis excursions (sensor-reported `vibration_kurtosis` reaches ~80 against a median of ~2.7). These are genuine impulsive events in the signal. **They are not labelled and their physical cause is unknown** -- they could be a mechanical impact, a loose mount, or a sensor artefact. They are left in the data and are simply the windows the anomaly detector should find unusual.

## 8. Window geometry chosen

Measured yield for candidate settings (a window is only emitted when all three sensors have >= 8 frames covering >= 60% of the window span):

| window | step | windows | segments covered | median frames/sensor | rejected | selected |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10 s | 5 s | 118 | 35 | 20 | 3 |  |
| 10 s | 2.5 s | 225 | 37 | 20 | 3 | **yes** |
| 8 s | 2 s | 305 | 42 | 16 | 22 |  |
| 15 s | 5 s | 84 | 24 | 30 | 0 |  |
| 20 s | 5 s | 60 | 20 | 40 | 0 |  |
| 6 s | 2 s | 342 | 47 | 12 | 36 |  |

**Selected: 10 s window, 2.5 s step (75% overlap).** Reasoning:

- 10 s at the measured 2 Hz gives ~20 frames per sensor, about the minimum for a usable kurtosis / percentile estimate.

- Longer windows (15 s, 20 s) collapse the dataset to 84 and 60 windows and cover only 24 and 20 of the 78 segments, because the median segment is only 8.07 s.

- Shorter windows (6 s, 8 s) push frames per sensor down to 12-16 and reject 36 and 22 candidate windows instead of 3.

- The step was reduced from the 5 s starting point to 2.5 s, which raises the sample count from 118 to 225. **These windows overlap by 75%, so they are correlated and are not 225 independent samples.** The effective independent count is nearer the 118 windows a 5 s step gives, and nearer still to the 37 distinct segments they come from.
